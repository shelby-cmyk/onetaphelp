How to choose a medical alert system.

8 minute read · Updated 2026

The medical alert industry has a problem: every company sounds the same. "24/7 monitoring." "Trusted brand." "Fast response." So how do you actually choose?

Here are the 7 questions that separate the good systems from the ones you'll regret.

1. Is the monitoring center US-based?

This is the most important question and the one most companies dance around. When you press the button, who actually answers? Some "US-based" companies use offshore call centers for overflow. Others route to a third party. Get a direct answer in writing.

Look for: TMA Five Diamond Certification. It's the highest accreditation a monitoring center can earn — only ~5% of centers in the US qualify.

2. Is there a long-term contract?

Life Alert requires a 3-year contract with cancellation fees up to $1,500. Most modern providers don't. Month-to-month service is standard now. If a company won't sell you month-to-month, walk away.

3. What's the total first-year cost?

The advertised monthly price is rarely the full picture. Add up:

  • Monthly service × 12
  • One-time activation/setup fee
  • Equipment cost (if purchased) or lease
  • Shipping
  • Optional add-ons (fall detection, key safe, extra buttons)

Compare apples to apples. A "$19.95/month" plan with a $245 setup fee, $19 shipping, and $10/month fall detection adds up to $658 in year one. A "$39.95/month" plan with everything included is $479.40.

4. Is fall detection included or extra?

Most companies charge $10/month extra for automatic fall detection. Read the fine print: some bundle it, others don't. If your loved one has had a fall — or a balance issue — fall detection isn't optional.

Important: No fall detection system is 100% accurate. It catches most falls but misses some. Always tell users to press the button manually whenever possible.

5. Does it work without a landline?

If the home doesn't have a landline (or only has VoIP), you need a cellular-based system. Most modern providers offer this — but the basic plan often requires a landline. Ask before you buy.

6. What's the actual response time?

Industry benchmark is under 30 seconds from button-press to a live agent. Best providers average under 20 seconds. Anything over 60 seconds is unacceptable. Ask for the company's published average response time, not their "fastest possible" time.

7. What's the return / cancellation policy?

The industry standard is a 30-day money-back guarantee. Read it carefully:

  • Does it cover the setup fee, or only the equipment cost?
  • Is there a restocking fee?
  • Who pays return shipping?
  • What about cancellation after 30 days — any penalty?

3 red flags that mean walk away

  • They won't tell you the price on the phone or website. If you have to "speak to a representative" to get a quote, the price is negotiable — and it's not in your favor.
  • They require a multi-year contract. Modern providers don't. If they need 3 years to make the math work, find another company.
  • Aggressive sales tactics. If the rep keeps offering "today only" deals, transferring you to a "manager," or telling you the price will go up if you don't decide now — hang up.

The bottom line

A good medical alert system is one your loved one will actually wear. That means it has to be:

  • Comfortable to wear (small, waterproof, attractive)
  • Easy to use (one button, no confusion)
  • Reliable (no false negatives, fast response)
  • Affordable enough that you can keep it for years

The technology is mature. Most major brands work fine. The differences are in the contracts, the pricing, and the experience — and those are the things you should be evaluating.
